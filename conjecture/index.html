  • The Compression Revolution: The Real Way AI Changes Your Brain

    29 November 2025 · Video, 31K views

  • The Articulation Engineering: How to Explain Complex Thoughts

    24 December 2025 · Video, 7.9K views

How the essays are made

An AI system I built drafts the essays. The questions are mine: each one starts as an intuition or a problem I'm living through. The AI Anxiety Paradox began as my own AI FOMO. I brief the system with tests drawn from Deutsch: is the explanation hard to vary? Does it reach beyond the case it was built for? What would refute it? It researches, proposes candidate explanations and criticizes them, and I choose what gets asked and what gets published.

Here is criticism at work on that essay. My final critic agent rejected its first submission at 9.1/10, with five complaints. One was that the line between pattern work and explanatory work was "undefined, circular". The rewrite answered all five, among other things by adding a test anyone can apply ("Can you write a complete specification for the task before starting?"), and the critic approved it.

The system, Content Factory, is public on GitHub. It judges a draft by whether it survives criticism, not by who wrote it. Its quality bars are frozen, so it can't lower them to let a draft through, although, as the last section shows, it found another way.

Every failure my checks catch in my agent loops goes into an append-only log: what the check saw, my best explanation of why, and the lesson. One entry, trimmed:

Saw: a scheduled run spent its whole $1 budget in 39 seconds, reported success, and produced nothing.

Why: the agent saved all its output for the end, so the budget ran out first. The runner trusted the "success" code instead of checking for output.

Lesson (the third time this failure recurred): write results as you go, and judge a run by what it produced, never by what it says about itself.

That last line is the principle I keep coming back to: a system's report on itself is a claim to be tested, not a test it has passed.

Where my work is weakest

  • Numbers where they don't belong. My pipeline scores drafts out of 10 (a draft needs 9.3 from the editor and 9.5 from the final critic). A Popperian would object that criticism can expose errors but can't add up to positive support for an idea, and a high score looks like exactly that kind of support. I treat the scores as tripwires for errors the checks can detect, not as degrees of confidence, but the tension is real and I haven't resolved it.

  • My own gate let a draft through. Preparing this page, I found that the critic reported the rewritten AI Anxiety Paradox as 9.5, its pass mark, but its own weighted scores add up to 9.4. It rounded itself over its own bar. And in March 2026 I added newer AI results to the published essay by hand, without sending it back through the critic. The checker needs checking too.

  • Uneven self-criticism. Two of the essays above state what would refute them. The Compression Revolution makes testable predictions but never says which outcomes it would accept as refuting it, and The Articulation Engineering states no refutation conditions at all.
